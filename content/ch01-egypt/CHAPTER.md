# Chapter 1 · Egypt

Five levels, south to north along the Nile, from Abu Simbel to the Delta. Three exist.

| Level | Site | Period the level draws on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Great Temple of Abu Simbel | Ramesses II, about 1264–1244 BC, and the relocation of 1964–68 |

| 3 | Karnak | Two thousand years of building; the Hypostyle Hall of Seti I and Ramesses II; the collapse of 1899; the Cachette of 1903 |

## The tone

Deadpan and mean. The world lies; the controls never do. Every death looks like what
caused it and nothing reacts. There is no text inside a level. The full rules are in
`PILLARS.md` at the repository root; read them before drawing a trap, because a trap
that needs a rule bent is the wrong trap.

## The tourist

A lost tourist in a costume bought at the airport: a striped bath towel worn as a
nemes, a false beard on an elastic, a loud shirt, shorts, socks and sandals. Nobody in
the game ever mentions it. It never changes the hitbox. The point of the costume is that
everything around the tourist is real and the tourist is not. The more exact the temples
are, the better the joke. That is why the history matters.

## What "accurate" means here

- The **architecture** is drawn as it stands today unless the level says otherwise:
  Abu Simbel relocated, Philae on Agilkia, Karnak with its unfinished pylon.
- The **sculpture** is drawn as it was made, because the game's statues are the
  finished statues: crowns, beards, cartouches, the family at the king's legs.
- **Modern intrusions** are shown honestly when they are the joke: the numbered
  relocation blocks, the cofferdam, the turnstile, the spotlights of the sound-and-light
  show. They must be as accurate as the temples.
- Every asset note separates **must be right** from **deliberately wrong**. There is no
  third category.

## Chapter-wide art

`shared/` holds the tourist (living, dead, sitting), the ankh block and the numbered
block. Those appear in every level and are painted once.
