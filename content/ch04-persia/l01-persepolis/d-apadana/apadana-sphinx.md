## What it is

One of the two seated sphinxes at the top of the central panel of the Apadana's east stair, facing
the winged disc between them: "a traditional representation of a winged sun, flanked by two
sphinxes" (Livius, through a search extract). In the game: a lion's body, seated, a bearded man's
head under a crown, a wing curving up from the shoulder and a forepaw raised toward the disc.
Frame 0 faces right (the left-hand sphinx) and frame 1 faces left (the right-hand one), each lit
from the upper right.

## Where it stands in the game

On the central projection's upper panel, sitting on the plain band at y 78, either side of the
winged disc. The left one is painted with its top-left at (1160, 62), frame 0; the right one at
(1190, 62), frame 1. The figure is the right-hand 16 × 16 of the painting; the left column and the
bottom row, which lies on the band, are its shadow. Not solid. **Never animated and never a gag:**
the ruling on the winged disc binds what frames it (`arc.md`, section 4). Nothing in the upper
panel ever moves; the trick is under the band. The two are one carving in its two facings
(pillar 4).

## Must be right

- Seated, facing the disc. A lion's body, a man's head, bearded and crowned, and a wing.
- Unpainted grey limestone, crisp, as carved as everything else on the wall.
- Nothing about them points at the trick or at the disc. They are carvings, and stay carvings.

## Deliberately wrong

- **Their size and their place in the panel** are the game's, fitted to a projection 104 px wide.

## Sources

Livius, the Apadana's east stairs (as `apadana-guard`), and its picture "Central frieze, Sphinx".
The tour map's research, `tools/monument-painters/specs/apadana.json`, and the plate's note,
`content/map/monuments/map-monument-ch04-persia.md`. Schmidt, *Persepolis I* (1953): reference.

## Confidence

Two seated sphinxes flanking the winged disc: search-extract level (Livius). **Not verified, so
ask before painting:** their pose (whether a forepaw is raised), their crowns and their wings. The
game follows the map plate in these, and the plate's note marks its sphinxes' pose and crowns as
the painter's, not the research's.
