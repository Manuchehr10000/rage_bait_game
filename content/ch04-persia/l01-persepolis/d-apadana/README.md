# Beat d · the Apadana's east court

Down the Tachara's stair into the court; the clock starts as he comes in. The back wall is the
Apadana's east stair under its modern shelter: delegations walking right along the south wing,
the left flight's lion leaping on a bull among cypresses, the central projection, the right
flight mirrored, Persian and Median nobles walking left along the north wing. Behind and above
it, broken columns on the Apadana's platform run off the top of the screen. In the centre, under
a small winged disc between two seated sphinxes, four guards a side face a blank. The court is
empty and the exit is beyond it. Run across, and as he passes the blank the guards step out of
the wall into the court, each where he is carved; he runs on into the right-hand file, and the
inner guard of that file catches him. He is left where he died, a stone figure of himself,
upright, in profile, facing the centre, drawn in front of the guards and pressed flat against
the one who caught him. **Death label: "The audience".** Let go of the key at the blank, inside
about four frames, and he stops in the king's place, the one place nobody steps into: they stand
round him, step back, and he walks on. Or stop short of the left-hand file, watch them go out
and back, and cross at once. The level's second trick and its last trap, on a cycle (pillar 5).
About 5 s, 0.83 of them standing.

The audience relief, the king on his throne giving audience, was taken out of the centre of this
stair in antiquity and set up in the Treasury, for reasons not known (Oxford Cabinet; Livius);
what stands there now is the panel the game draws, guards facing a blank. The stair lay under
fallen mud brick until Herzfeld dug it out in 1931–34, and is crisp for it; it has been under a
permanent shelter since the mid-1990s (Lonely Planet). The winged disc is a living community's
emblem: never animated, never a gag (`../../CHAPTER.md`, ruling 6). Before the guards are painted,
check their height, the blank's width, and which are Persian and which Median (LEVEL.md, "Not
verified"); until then all eight are one figure.

The guard has a note here: one figure, eight times, carved in the wall and standing out of it,
the same to the pixel. So do the façade's other carvings that the game repeats: the delegate,
the usher, the Persian noble, the Median noble, the cypress, the lion and bull, the sphinx and the
winged disc. The court's surface is beat b's tiles. The columns, the shelter, the façade's wall,
mouldings and plain band, the guards' stepping out and the carved death, which turns the
tourist's own frame to stone and draws it in front of the guards, are drawn by the game.
