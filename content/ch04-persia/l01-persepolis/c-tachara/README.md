# Beat c · the Tachara

Honest. A low stair up onto Darius's platform, built of the terrace's grey masonry; the frames
standing on it are a darker and more polished stone. Across it, two doorways in section: in each a stone lintel over him, its
underside 48 px above the platform, and on the far jamb the king walking out of his hall under a
parasol an attendant holds over him. On the west doorway he walks west, on the east doorway
east: on both he is leaving. On the platform a window block and a niche block stand alone, the
same from outside. A stair down the other side into the Apadana's court. A jump under a lintel
bumps it and nothing happens. Nothing here lies. About 3.6 s.

The Tachara is the "Hall of Mirrors", for its dark, polished limestone (Iranica; Livius). Its
mud-brick walls are gone and its stone frames stand on their own: sixteen windows and niches,
each cut from one block with an Egyptian cavetto cornice, one window block 2.65 × 2.65 × 1.70 m
and about 18 t; a door frame of three monoliths, about 75 t. The jambs carry the king with his
parasol bearer and, smaller, an attendant with a towel or fly-whisk, whom the game leaves out.
The platform is drawn 3.4 m high, against 2.2 to 3.0 m in the sources (LEVEL.md, "Deliberately
wrong").

The window block (one note for the window and the niche, which are the same from outside), the
king on the jamb and the lintels, which are polished tiles (with sky above, and the plain one the
level never shows), have notes here. The door jambs, the cornices on the lintels, the stairs and
the platform, which is the terrace's masonry (beat a's tiles), are drawn by the game.
