## What it is

The relief on the jambs of the Tachara's doorways: the king walking out of his hall, and behind
him an attendant, drawn smaller, holding a parasol over him. On the real jambs a second attendant,
also smaller, follows with a towel and a fly-whisk (Iranica, "Persepolis"; Livius). Cut in the
Tachara's dark polished stone. Frame 0 walks right and frame 1 walks left, each lit from the upper
right, so frame 1 is not frame 0 flipped.

## Where it stands in the game

On the far jamb of each of the Tachara's two doorways: at x 800 (tile 50), frame 1, walking west;
at x 864 (tile 54), frame 0, walking east. On both he is walking out of the hall that would stand
between the doorways. Painted with its top-left at (799, 44) and at (863, 44). The figure is the
right-hand 16 columns, which are exactly the jamb's width; the left column and the bottom row are
its shadow, down and to the left. The bottom row is at y 78, a pixel clear of the platform at y 80.
The jamb behind it, from the platform up to the lintel's underside at y 32, is drawn by the game.

Not solid; never animated. The two are one carving in its two facings, and otherwise **identical
to the pixel** (pillar 4).

## Must be right

- The king larger and in front; the attendant smaller, behind him, holding the parasol's pole so
  that its canopy is over the king's head.
- The king walks out of the hall, in profile, in the long Persian court robe, crowned and bearded.
- Cut in the polished stone of the jamb, unpainted, lit from the upper right.
- As carved as everything else in the level: a carving that stays a carving
  (`../LEVEL.md`, "The trick").

## Deliberately wrong

- **One attendant.** The second, with the towel and the fly-whisk, is left out. A painter may put
  him back inside the jamb's 16 columns (`../LEVEL.md`).
- **The size.** The king is about 27 px, about 2.9 m with his crown, on a jamb 48 px high, and the
  parasol fills the space above him. The real figures' size is not verified.
- **The far jamb only.** The game draws the jamb behind the doorway; the near one is cut away with
  the section (`tile-persepolis-polished-top`).

## Confidence

The king with a parasol bearer, and a smaller attendant with a towel and fly-whisk, on the
Tachara's doorjambs: search-extract level. **Not verified, so ask before painting:** the king's
crown, his robe and what he holds; which jambs carry the relief, and which way he walks on each;
the figures' size against the jamb; the parasol's shape; where the second attendant walks.
