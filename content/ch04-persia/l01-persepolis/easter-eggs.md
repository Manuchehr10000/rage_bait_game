# Easter eggs · Persepolis

For the designer. None of it is stated anywhere in the game.

- **He is given the audience.** The answer to the trap is to stand in the blank, in the middle of
  the panel that took the place of the king's audience, taken out in antiquity. For six tenths of
  a second the guards step out and stand round a man in a clip-on beard, facing him, and nobody
  steps into his place. Then they go back into the wall and he walks off. Nobody mentions it.

- **He joins them.** The death leaves him in stone where he died, upright, in profile, facing the
  centre, pressed flat against the guard who caught him: one more figure on the stair. Bearded
  men are carved all along it, and his is the only beard among them that is clipped on.

- **Every step is a pixel.** At his scale a 10 cm riser is about one pixel and a 31 cm tread
  about three, so the stair is drawn step for step and its slope of 1 in 3 is the real pitch. A
  teacher who counts the first flight finds 64 steps, one more than the real 63, and 48 on the
  second.

- **He runs up a stair made for walking slowly.** The steps were cut low, the likelier reading
  goes, so that a visitor climbed at a dignified pace, and not, as the story has it, so that
  horses could ride up (Livius). He goes up at a run.

- **A stair Darius probably never climbed.** The Stairs of All Nations were built under Xerxes; before
  them the main way onto the terrace was from the south (Livius). The level's way up is the
  later one.

- **The names.** More than two hundred foreign visitors cut their names at Persepolis from the
  seventeenth century on, most of them on the Gate and the Apadana; Stanley's is among them, dated
  1870 (Iranica, "Persepolis graffiti"). The scratches on the Gate's piers are never legible, so
  any of them could be his. The tourist cuts nothing. He walks through, which is what a gate is
  for.

- **Windows and niches.** The Tachara's windows and its niches are identical from outside,
  each one block under the same cornice. The game's rule that identical things stay identical
  (pillar 4) was kept here first by Darius's masons.

- **The king is leaving.** On both of the Tachara's doorways the king on the far jamb walks out of
  his hall, under a parasol somebody else holds. At the first doorway he is walking out toward the
  tourist; at the second, out ahead of him, the same way. The tourist has no parasol.

- **Under a roof.** The guards step out under the shelter put over the east stair in the
  mid-1990s, to keep off it the weather it was spared while it lay buried under fallen mud
  brick, until the 1930s.

- **The gecko.** *Cyrtopodion persepolense* (Nazarov et al. 2009) is known from nowhere but
  Persepolis, on its vertical walls with crevices. It is 3.4 to 5.1 cm from snout to vent and
  about 7 to 10.6 cm with its tail, never more than one world pixel (10.6 cm at 9.4 px a metre),
  so it is on every wall in the level and cannot be drawn on any of them.
