## What it is

A Minoan column as every visitor sees one, which means as Evans rebuilt it: a stone base, a
shaft that tapers **downward**, a black cushion capital and a square abacus under the beam, a
storey high. No Minoan column survives anywhere at Knossos: the taper, the capital and the
colours come from frescoes, seals, the stone bases and the Mycenaean stone versions.

## Where it stands in the game

Every column in the level is this painting, at the same size: four of Evans's in the storeroom,
the one at the head of the Grand Staircase, A and C beside its light well; and the two that
fail — the fifth in the storeroom (the last burnt Minoan one) and B (Fyfe's timber of 1901).
**They must be identical to the pixel** (pillar 4). The game draws the failing ones folding —
the plaster splitting, a charred or rotten core, the shaft going down into its base — itself.
The top of the painting is the underside of the slab it carries; the bottom row is the floor.

## Must be right

- The downward taper, the cushion capital, the abacus, the stone base.
- Red shaft and black capital, as the level draws them.

## Deliberately wrong

- Columns in a storeroom: none ever stood there; Evans's 1929 roof rests on the storeroom walls.
- A Minoan column still standing: none survives.
- One colour scheme for every column in the level.

## Sources

Knossos Documenta, reconstruction work and paint restoration (as `tile-knossos-slab`). The
column of the Lion Gate at Mycenae for the shape.

## Confidence

The shape: solid. **The colour of the columns in each area (red shaft and black capital, or the
reverse) is not verified. Ask before painting.**
