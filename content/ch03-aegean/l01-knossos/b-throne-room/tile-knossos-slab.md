## What it is

Evans's reinforced concrete, seen on edge: every roof and upper floor in the level. He cast the
upper storeys, roofs and column capitals in concrete from the 1920s, plastered their faces and
painted on them the timber frames they replaced: a beam along the slab, and under it a row of
round beam ends.

## Where it stands in the game

The Throne Room's ceiling, and the floors of the upper storeys either side of the Grand
Staircase's light well. None of it ever falls: at Knossos everything of Evans's holds. Nothing
on the tile may ever differ from one slab to the next (pillar 4).

## Must be right

- A plastered face, a painted timber beam along it, painted round beam ends below it.
- It reads as Evans's: a 1920s reconstruction of Minoan timber framing, not the real thing.

## Deliberately wrong

- The beam and beam ends are the same on every tile, spaced by the tile, not by any real bay.

## Sources

Knossos Documenta, reconstruction work: http://knossosdocumenta.gr/en/reconstruction-work/ and
paint restoration: https://knossosdocumenta.gr/en/paint-restoration/ . Ashmolean Museum,
*Rebuilding the Palace of Minos at Knossos*.

## Confidence

The concrete, the painted timber and the round beam ends: solid. The colours of the painted
beams in each part of the palace: not verified. Look before painting.
