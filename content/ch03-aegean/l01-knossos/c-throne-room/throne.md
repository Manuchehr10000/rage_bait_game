## What it is

The gypsum throne of the Throne Room, found in April 1900 and still where it was found: one
block of gypsum, a high back with a wavy top, a seat hollowed for a body, the front a pair of
arched supports. Evans put a wooden copy of it in the anteroom. **This painting is both.**

## Where it stands in the game

The original against the Throne Room's wall, where a full jump out of the basin comes down; the
copy in the anteroom, where a full jump over the barrier comes down. The seat's footprint on the
floor is the whole width of the seat. Landing in front of the original seats him and his visit
is over; the copy does nothing. Bottom edge on the floor.

## Must be right

- One block of gypsum; the wavy top of the back; the hollowed seat; the arched front.
- It is the original, in place. Visitors look at it from behind a barrier and do not go in.
- The game takes no side on whose seat it was (a king's, in Evans's reading; a priestess's or a
  goddess's, argued since). Only the tourist ever sits there.

## Deliberately wrong

- **Evans's copy is the colour of the gypsum.** It is wood; it is painted to match here so that
  the two thrones are the same to the pixel (pillar 4).
- The throne seats him. It is a chair and has never done anything to anybody.

## Sources

Knossos Documenta, Throne Room: https://knossosdocumenta.gr/en/throne-room/ . Evans, *The Palace
of Minos* IV (1935).

## Confidence

The throne, its material and its find date: solid. **Who made the anteroom copy and when, and
whether visitors once sat in it for photographs: not verified.**
