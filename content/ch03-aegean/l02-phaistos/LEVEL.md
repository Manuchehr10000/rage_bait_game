# Chapter 3 · Level 2 · Phaistos

A ridge at the western end of the Messara plain in south Crete, with Mount Ida (Psiloritis)
to the north and, high on its flank, the dark mouth of the Kamares cave. The second palace
of Minoan Crete, built twice. The **Old Palace** of about 1900 BC stood on terraces cut into
the hillside and was destroyed, most likely by earthquakes, at the end of the Protopalatial
period. The **New Palace** was built over it and a little to the east, on the Old Palace
levelled and sealed: about 1.3 m of fill over the Old Palace's west court, the new west
façade set about 7 m further back, and Old Palace rooms half-filled with a rock-hard mass
that the New Palace stands on. What that mass is — a lime concrete poured on purpose, or the
Old Palace's own collapse fused by fire and water — is disputed.

It was dug by the Italian mission: Federico Halbherr and **Luigi Pernier** from 1900, and
**Doro Levi** from 1950 to 1971, who went down through the New Palace into the Old and found
three phases of it. Nobody rebuilt anything. The ruins were conserved as they were found, and
the only roofs on the site are **five metal shelters of the 1960s** over the most exposed
remains: the Royal Apartments, part of the Old Palace, the kiln, the West Bastion and Room
LXXXIX. Their replacements were approved in 2025 and funded in 2026 and are not yet built.
They hold up nothing but a roof.

The **Phaistos Disc** was found on 3 July 1908 in the North-East Complex, by Pernier's
foreman on an evening inspection. It is in the Heraklion Museum, and it is not in this level.

## What this level is for

Level 2 turns level 1's lessons round. Knossos killed from above and the light caught every
landing; here nothing is overhead but five sheds that never fall, and every death is a fall.

> Knossos: the fake parts hold.
> Phaistos: nothing falls on you. You fall.

Every fall in the level lands on the same thing, and the museum label says so: all its
deaths have one noun, **The Old Palace**.

What it turns round:

- **Knossos: one storey is nothing, and the light catches every landing.** Phaistos teaches
  the chapter's count: walk off two storeys and live, jump off two and die, walk off three and
  die. The light is everywhere, and it catches nothing.
- **Knossos: look up.** Its dangers were the floors over his head. Phaistos is drawn in section
  and the danger is under his feet: **look down.**
- **Knossos: columns hold the floor up, and the liar stands beside the light.** Phaistos has
  no columns, only bases with nothing on them. What holds its floor up is under it: the walls
  of the Old Palace. Stand where an old wall is under your feet.
- **Knossos: wait in the middle of the room, never at a door.** At Phaistos only the doors'
  bases survive. The last thing the level asks is to stop on a door base and wait.
- **Knossos: go down into the light.** At Phaistos the light well goes down without you.

About **24 seconds clean.** The first clean run should cost five to nine deaths.

## The two tells the player reads

Phaistos is readable, as a level 2 should be. Both tells are drawn by the level itself and
need no change to the engine.

- **Whether you can see the bottom.** The Upper West Court is laid at the top of the level,
  where the camera stops rising, so the ground a storey below its first edge is on screen and
  the ground two storeys below its second is not. Anywhere lower in the level the camera shows
  only about 73 px under his feet, less than a storey, so the rule the level teaches holds
  everywhere after it: **if you can see the bottom you may jump; if you cannot, walk.** Walking
  off is safe down to two storeys.
- **The treads.** A storey is five treads, as the Grand Staircase at Knossos taught. A stair
  beside an edge says how far down it goes.
- **The section.** From the west wing on, the ground under the New Palace floor is drawn cut
  open: the Old Palace's walls in stone, its rooms either sealed with the hatched rock-hard
  mass or hollow where Levi dug them out. The slab and the top of the room below it are always
  on screen.

### a · The two West Courts

He arrives on foot (pillar 13) at the top of the level, and the site's name stands on its
museum label for two seconds (pillar 2). Ida and the Kamares cave are behind everything.

1. **The first edge.** Beside the visitors' steps, the path drops one storey to the Upper West
   Court. The ground is in sight, five treads down. Walk off or jump, he lives. It confirms
   what Knossos taught, once.
2. **The Upper West Court**, paving in full sun, and at its far end the high retaining wall
   over the Lower West Court: an edge pixel-identical to the first, and two storeys down. The
   ground is out of sight. Beside it, ten treads of the old stair, and the theatral area's
   broad steps against the wall.
   - **First attempt.** He jumps, as the first edge and all of Knossos taught him. A full jump
     off two storeys is 222 px from the top of the arc, and he dies on the court below. Label:
     **The Old Palace.**
   - **Second attempt.** He walks off (155 px) or walks down the stair, and lives.
   - A tap-hop off the wall is about 172 px and lives: what kills is the full, committed jump.
     Pillar 1 already rules that the height of a jump is the player's own (the first pylon at
     Karnak).

The first two edges are identical and one storey apart (pillar 4), and the player learns the
count by the only means the game has: dying once and remembering.

### b · The Old Palace's court and the Grand Staircase

The level's one breather, and it is full of things to look at.

- **Four round pits**, open, hopped. Knossos had three; the player who has played it flinches
  at the fourth, and it is only a pit.
- **An old raised walkway** crossing the court at an angle, running straight under the wall of
  the New Palace: the Old Palace's path, which the new building was set down on top of.
- **The Old Palace's west façade**, low, and above and behind it the New Palace's.
- **The Grand Staircase**, about 14 m wide, a dozen steps, walked and never a trap.
- **The Propylon** at the top: the base of one great column with nothing on it. The whole spine
  in one picture: nobody holds anything up.

### c · The storey you cannot see

The New Palace floor, gypsum slabs in the sun, every one the same. Under it, in section, the
Old Palace: stone walls, rooms sealed with the rock-hard mass drawn hatched, and rooms Levi dug
out, hollow, dropping to the lower terrace.

- A slab over an old wall or over the mass holds.
- A slab over a hollow room tips a beat (about 0.15 s) after his foot is on it, pivoting off
  the wall toward the hollow, and he falls free, three storeys. Label: **The Old Palace.**
- The hollow spans are wider than he can cross in that beat at a run, so running is not an
  answer. Crossing them in the air, and coming down over a wall, is.
- **First attempt.** He runs, and the first hollow span tips under him.
- **Second attempt.** He reads the ground under the floor, and jumps from wall to wall.
- The floors tip; they do not ride him down. He falls free and the plain 200 px rule kills
  him, so the level does not depend on the open ruling about floors that drop with him
  (`arc.md`, section 4). Never use a floor's own kill cause at Phaistos: it would kill at any
  height and break the count.

This is the turn of Knossos's storerooms. There a footfall under the original brought down
what was over him; here a footfall on the new takes away what is under him. There columns
held his floor up from above; here the old walls hold it from below.

### d · The Royal Apartments

The finale. Its only wait is the level's only wait (pillar 5).

1. Two seconds across the Central Court in full sun, Ida ahead. Its south-east corner has gone
   down the hill; it is in the backdrop.
2. Past the fence into the hall of the north wing, under the 1960s shelter: shade, gypsum
   floors with red plaster in the joints, and the door partitions surviving as bases set in the
   floor, with nothing standing on them. The shelter holds up a roof and nothing else, and it
   never moves.
3. Beyond the last base, the light well, open to the sky, the brightest floor in the level:
   the first thing since Knossos that looks like where a jump lands safely. In section there is
   nothing under it. The well is wider than a full jump, and the wall past it is sheer: the way
   on is down.
4. **The timeline** starts when his centre crosses the near edge of the last base (a position,
   not a footfall, so a jump over the base cannot skip it). About 0.4 s later the light well's
   floor tips off its bearers and the Old Palace floors under it follow, one after the other,
   three storeys. At about 1.2 s they come to rest as one heap, its top two storeys down.
   - **First attempt.** He runs into the light, as Knossos taught him, and goes down with it,
     three storeys. Label: **The Old Palace.**
   - **Second attempt.** He stops on the base — at the door, where Knossos's doors taught him
     never to stand — and watches it go. Then the count, one last time: walking off onto the
     heap is about 155 px and he lives; jumping onto it is about 222 px and he dies; walking
     off before it has settled is three storeys and he dies.
   - The heap lands as one solid at a fixed time, so a walk-off is either before it or after
     it, never in between.
5. The lethal edge is in the open, under the sky, not under the shelter, so a jump is never
   cut short by a roof.

It is the chapter's multi-storey collapse turned downward. At Knossos a collapse landed one
storey above him and Evans's floor caught it; here nothing catches anything, and he is the one
who has to be somewhere else.

### e · Out through the Old Palace

Along the bottom, under the New Palace's floor: the storey he could not see, seen, and the
only floor over his head in the whole level. Out past the rope at the North-East Complex, where
the Old Palace shows and the Disc was found. Nothing happens.

## Deliberate lies

- **The heights.** The visitors' steps as one storey and the Upper Court's wall as two; the
  Old Palace three storeys under the New in the west wing and the north wing. The real fill
  over the Old Palace's court is about 1.3 m, and the Old Palace lies about one level under the
  New. A storey drawn at 80 px is roughly double a Minoan storey.
- **The first edge is a drop.** Visitors go down steps.
- **The New Palace floor is still laid over rooms Levi dug.** Where he dug, the floor was lifted
  and the pits are open. The floor tips; it never did anything.
- **The tipping, the light well's collapse and the heap.** Nothing at Phaistos has fallen since
  the Bronze Age except the hillside, slowly. The heap is the level's own collapse and makes no
  claim about the rock-hard mass.
- **The light well is wider than a jump**, has nothing under it, and the shelter stops at the
  last door base so the well is open to the sun (whether the shelter covers the light well today
  is not verified).
- **He goes past the fence.** The Royal Apartments are closed to visitors, and so is the Old
  Palace's west wing; the level walks him through both. He is where nobody is allowed (pillar 11).
- **The way out along the Old Palace under the north wing.** Old Palace remains are recorded
  under the New Palace and in the north-east corner; the passage under the north wing is the
  level's.
- **The Grand Staircase is walked.** Whether visitors may climb it today is reported both ways.
- **The court is crossed in two seconds**, its eroded south-east only in the backdrop.
- **Every death has one noun.** A museum label would name the court, the rooms and the well
  separately; this one names what they all land on.
- The kouloures' spacing and depth, the walkway's angle and every distance are layout
  (`arc.md`, invariants).

## What is true and is doing the work

- The New Palace stands on the Old, levelled and sealed; the Old Palace's west façade stands
  below and west of the New one's; its walkway runs under the new walls.
- Two West Courts, upper and lower, and visitors come down to them from the north-west.
- Four round pits of the Old Palace in its court, covered when the New Palace court was laid,
  open again today.
- The Grand Staircase and the Propylon with its great column base.
- Nobody rebuilt Phaistos. Levi dug down into the Old Palace.
- Five 1960s metal shelters over the most exposed remains, holding up nothing but a roof.
- The Royal Apartments: alabaster floors with red joints, the door partitions as bases in the
  floor, a light well, a colonnade facing Ida, fenced off and under a shelter.
- The Central Court looks north to Ida, and its south-east has gone down the hill.
- Ida and the mouth of the Kamares cave are visible from the palace; the Old Palace's fine
  pottery is named Kamares ware after the cave.

## Not in the level

- **The Phaistos Disc.** It is writing (pillar 2), it is in Heraklion, and a joke about signs
  nobody can read drifts toward a joke about the culture. Found on 3 July 1908 by Pernier's
  foreman (named in the sources as Zakarias Iliakis or Eliakis), in Pernier's space 8 of room 101
  of the North-East Complex, in a black destruction layer disturbed in Hellenistic times, with the
  Linear A tablet PH 1 nearby. Generally accepted as genuine; Eisenberg's hoax claim of 2008 was
  answered by Hnila in 2009. Undeciphered (`arc.md`, section 7).
- **What the rock-hard mass is.** Poured lime concrete or fused collapse; the level draws it as a
  visitor sees it and takes no side.
- **What the kouloures were for.** Granaries, offering pits, or neither (Strasser 1997).
- The theatral area's rows (eight today, perhaps nine once); the Central Court's length; the
  claim that the staircase's steps were sloped to shed rain, which only tourism pages make.
- The Hellenistic town over the North-East Complex; the villa at Hagia Triada a few kilometres
  west, dug by the same mission, whose painted sarcophagus gives the chapter its lyre.

## New in the engine

- The ground drawn in section under a floor: stone walls, the hatched mass, hollow rooms. Decor.
- Slabs that tip toward a hollow a beat after a footfall and let him fall free. The `tips` of the
  existing crumble, with no kill cause.
- The finale: a position trigger, a stack of floors that tip in turn, and a heap that lands as one
  solid at a fixed time, two storeys down.
- Theme `phaistos`: sun-worn gypsum, the Old Palace's stone, the 1960s metal shelter (a roof on
  posts that never falls), the fence and the rope.
- The level's top rows laid out so the camera, resting at the top, shows the first edge's ground
  and not the second's. No change to the camera.
- One `dropCause` for the level, **The Old Palace**.
- Walk-throughs: both edges walked and the first jumped live, the second jumped dies; a run across
  a hollow span dies, wall to wall lives; running into the light well dies, waiting on the base and
  walking off lives, walking off early dies, jumping onto the heap dies.

## Confidence

**How this was checked.** Web search only, and the network blocked page fetches, so almost every
claim comes from search extracts, mostly of guidebooks and news reports, with a few scholarly
abstracts. Nothing below is from reading Pernier, Levi or Graham. Read them before any asset note
is signed off.

**Solid.** The Upper and Lower West Courts and the visitors' way down to them; the Old Palace's
west façade below the New; the Royal Apartments fenced off under a 1960s shelter; five 1960s metal
shelters, and their replacement approved and funded but not built; the slope stabilisation and
rainwater works in the same programme; Ida visible to the north; Kamares ware named after the cave
and Levi's Old Palace deposits its reference sequence.

**Probable.** Four kouloures, Old Palace, covered in the New Palace period, open today; about 1.3 m
of fill over the Old court and the New façade about 7 m further east; the old walkways running under
the new buildings; the staircase about 14 m wide with a dozen steps; the Propylon's great column
base; the Central Court about 22 m wide, looking to Ida, its south-east lost to erosion; the Royal
Apartments' alabaster floors with red joints, the partitions as bases, the light well in the corner;
the Disc found by the foreman on 3 July 1908.

**Discrepant or contested.** The rows of the theatral area (8 or 9); the Central Court's length
(46 m or 63 m); whether the Old Palace stood on three terraces or used two of three; whether the
staircase is open to visitors; what the rock-hard mass is; what the kouloures were for; whether the
court's south-east went to erosion (usual) or an earthquake (one tourism page).

**Not verified, so ask before painting:**
- whether the Royal Apartments' light well is under the shelter today;
- which rooms the Old Palace shelter covers, and what the 1960s shelters look like (posts, roof);
- whether the kouloures are stone-lined;
- what Old Palace remains lie under the north wing;
- where exactly the rope at the North-East Complex runs.
