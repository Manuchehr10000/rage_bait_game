## What it is

Chapter 3's costume, from the research: "bull-leaper kit, badly tied and slipping". *Kit* in the
sporting sense: he has come dressed for the sport. Frames in order: **idle**, **walk1**,
**walk2**, **jump**. Faces right; the game flips it.

- **His own clothes:** a lime running vest, navy running shorts, white trainers. A different
  outline from chapter 1's fleece and boots and chapter 2's shirt, socks and sandals.
- **The shop's kit over them:** a black wig with a long lock down his back, held on by a white
  terry sweatband across his brow; a gold plastic belt; a short red kilt with a blue edge; a
  gold band round his upper arm.
- **The one thing that slips is the belt.** The men in Minoan pictures wear the belt high and
  tight at the waist. His has slid down to his hips, with the vest bunched above it, and the
  kilt hangs a pixel lower at the back, where the knot is not. Nothing holds it up. The wig does
  not slip, because the sweatband is holding it on; that is what the sweatband is for.
- **In the jump** the kilt flies up and the running shorts show.
- **A tuft of his own hair**, sandy, shows in front of the lock. It is the same hair that shows
  when the wig comes off (`bull-leaper-dead`, `bull-leaper-seated`).

## Where it stands in the game

Every level of chapter 3, and on the tour map while the Aegean is under the cursor. 12 × 16
with a 10 × 16 hitbox: the sprite is drawn one pixel left of the hitbox, so keep the body inside
the middle 10 columns and let only the lock and the nose use the outer pixel on each side. The
feet are at the bottom row.

## Must be right

- **Visibly bought and visibly his.** The wig is a wig, the belt is plastic, the kilt is a
  costume-shop kilt over running shorts. It mocks the tourist, never the fresco or the people
  in it (pillar 11). A history teacher must see the joke, not a bad Minoan.
- **The real thing, so that the fake can be wrong on purpose:** in Minoan painting young men
  wear their hair long, in locks; a belt drawn tight at the waist; a short kilt or loincloth;
  bands on the arm and wrist. Look at the Bull-Leaping ("Toreador") Fresco and the other
  Knossos figures before painting, and paint the costume shop's version of them.
- **No skin paint, ever.** Aegean painting shows its figures red or white by a convention of
  the art; the costume never imitates it, and neither does his skin.
- **No pinched waist drawn on his body.** The tight belt is the Minoans'; his has slipped.
- **No bull on anything he wears**: no horns, no bull print, no bull on the vest. Levels 1 to
  5 have no bull at all (`content/research/arc.md`, section 4). The chapter's refusal of the
  bull is the setup, and the Minotaur of level 6 is the punchline: he arrives at it dressed to
  leap a bull and is not the one who gets anybody out (pillar 11). The costume must still be
  the same costume there.
- It never changes the hitbox (pillar 9). Nobody in the game ever mentions it.
- Skin, face and his own hair colour are the designer's call. The tourist is nobody in
  particular.

## Deliberately wrong

The whole costume. Also:

- The kilt's colours and the belt's gold are the costume shop's, not read off any fresco.
- The fresco the costume copies was itself restored from fragments for Evans, as all the
  Knossos frescoes were (the chapter's error dossier). A costume of a restoration is the
  chapter's own joke about Knossos, and stays in this note.
