# Chapter 1 · Level 2 · Roc-aux-Sorciers

Angles-sur-l'Anglin, Vienne. A rock shelter at the foot of a limestone cliff on the bank
of the river Anglin, facing south and lit by the sun all day. Two parts adjoin: the
**Abri Bourdois**, a shallow shelter with a single mostly sunlit chamber, and the deeper
**Cave Taillebourg** beside it. On the back wall of the Bourdois runs a sculpted frieze
about 20 m long and 2.6 m high, carved in the Middle Magdalenian and unique in the world
for its scale: roughly thirty-four figures — ibex, horses, bison, felines, a reindeer,
human heads, and five female figures carved life size from the waist to the knees.

The site was found by Lucien Rousseau in 1927. The frieze itself came to light in 1950,
during the excavations Suzanne de Saint-Mathurin ran from 1947 to 1964 with Dorothy
Garrod — the first woman to hold a professorship at Cambridge. What saved the sculpture
is what ended the occupation: the roof came down, the shelter was abandoned and the
carvings lay sealed under the fall until the twentieth century dug them out.

The site has been closed to the public since it was classified a Historic Monument in
1955. The chapter does not go inside the interpretation centre and is not built around a
facsimile (`content/research/arc.md`, chapter 1, Gate 6). The gate in beat a is the only
acknowledgement that the tourist has no business being here at all.

## What the level teaches

Level 1 was dark, and everything the tourist could see was lying. This one is the other
way round. **Nothing on this wall changes its mind**: every figure cut deep enough to be
a ledge holds his weight, every time, however long he stands on it. What varies is
whether the light lets him tell which those are. The one dishonest thing in the level is
in the dark half, and it is not the wall — it is a piece of the wall that came off
seventeen thousand years ago and is leaning where it landed.

So: at Cap Blanc the committed jump was the death and the short hop was the way out. Here
the short hop is what puts you in the river.

## Deliberate lies

- **The Anglin runs under the whole frieze.** It does not. The shelter floor is dry and
  was dug out by hand between 1947 and 1964. The river is put where the trench was.
- **Three figures of the flat run are engraving and five are sculpture, in that order.**
  The mix is real; this arrangement of it is level design.
- **The horns of the confronting ibex bridge a gap and carry a man.** They meet on the
  real wall. They are a few centimetres of limestone and they carry nothing.
- **The blocks of the collapse sink under a standing man.** They came to rest in the
  Magdalenian and have not moved since. This is the level's one running pressure.
- **A block of the fall is still leaning, loose, in Cave Taillebourg**, seventeen
  thousand years later, exactly where a man would put his foot.
- The **scale**: the frieze here is longer and the animals are further apart than 20 m of
  real wall allows. Spacing and jump distances are level design (`arc.md`, invariants).

## Confidence

**Solid.** The two parts of the site and which is which. The frieze's length and height,
and the count of figures (8 ibex, 7 horses, 6 bison, 4 felines, 1 reindeer, 1
unidentified, 4 human heads, 5 female figures). The south-facing, sunlit, shallow
shelter on the Anglin. Rousseau in 1927, the frieze in 1950, Saint-Mathurin and Garrod
1947–64. The rockfall that ended the occupation and covered the sculpture. Ochre,
charcoal and manganese traces. Classified 1955, closed since.

**Watch the dating.** `arc.md` gives the chapter's figure, c. 15,000 BP, which is the one
in general use. The excavation literature dates the first occupation to 17.3–16.7 ka cal
BP and the rockfalls to 17.1–16.5 ka cal BP — calibrated, and so a larger number for the
same event. Both are in this file on purpose. Where the game needs one, use the
research's.

**Not verified, so not stated as fact anywhere in the art.** The exact composition of the
famous panel of women, and how many of the five belong to it. Whether the confronting
ibex face each other across the line a visitor walks. The origin of the name
*Roc-aux-Sorciers*. The shelter's exact distance below the village. If the designer needs
any of these, ask before painting them.
