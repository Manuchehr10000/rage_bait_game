## What it is

A block of the shelter roof, lying where it fell. Roughly rectangular, broken on every
face. What faces up is the **bedding plane** — the flat, dull back of the stone — because
the block landed on the side that was carved. At the bottom edge, where it is tipped
slightly, one or two spots of a **worked surface** show, turned under. 40 × 12.

## Where it stands in the game

Beat e, five of them a stride apart in the margin of the river. Each is a 40 × 12 solid
until somebody stands on it, and then it settles into the water at a constant 26 px a
second and does not stop.

## Must be right

- **Limestone, bedded**, the same stone as the wall it fell off. Cream, not grey.
- The top is a **bedding plane**: flat, natural, uninteresting. Any carving visible from
  above is wrong — the whole point is that the sculpture is face down in the mud.
- Broken faces are **fresh in profile and weathered on the old ceiling side**. A block
  that came off a roof has one face that used to be a ceiling.
- Wet at the bottom: it is standing in a river.
- No moss, no soil, no vegetation on the upper face.

## Deliberately wrong

It moves. Blocks of this fall came to rest around 17,000 years ago and have been still
ever since; the excavators lifted the ones that mattered and recorded the rest in place.
Nothing here sinks under anybody.

## Confidence

The collapse, its role in sealing the frieze, and the fact that sculpted blocks were found
in the deposit are all documented. The size and shape of this particular block are design.
