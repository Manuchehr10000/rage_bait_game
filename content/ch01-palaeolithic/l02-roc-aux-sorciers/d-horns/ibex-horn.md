# The horn of the confronting ibex

## What it is

One ibex horn in relief, reaching in **from the left with its tip at the right-hand end**,
tapering from about 10 px at the base to 3 at the point, and curving slightly. **Ridged
across its whole length**, every three pixels: the transverse ridges of a real ibex horn
are the reason this object is legible as a horn and not as a branch. 44 × 20.

The game draws it twice, the second mirrored, so the two tips meet in the middle of the
gap. Paint one; the mirror is free.

## Where it stands in the game

Beat d, spanning the 76 px between the two ibex, at the same height as the backs on either
side. The top edge of the horn is the floor: the collision is a 76 × 6 strip along the
line the two horns make. The player walks across it.

## Must be right

- **The ridges.** Evenly spaced, across the curve, standing proud. Without them this is
  not an ibex horn.
- The taper is from the head outward, so the thick end is the one attached to the animal
  and the point is the one over the void.
- It is **carved in relief on a wall**, not a horn in the air: it has a lit upper edge and
  a shadow under it, like every other figure here.
- The two horns must read as belonging to the two animals either side, not as a beam
  someone laid across.

## Deliberately wrong

Two ibex horns do not span three quarters of a metre of empty wall, they do not meet, and
they do not carry a grown man. The confronting pair is a real motif; this bridge is the
game's.

## Sources

- Ministère de la Culture, *Les abris sculptés de la Préhistoire*, Roc-aux-Sorciers, for
  the ibex of the frieze.
- A live *Capra ibex* for the ridging and the taper.

## Confidence

The ridging and the ibex's prominence here are solid. **Not verified:** whether the real
confronting pair face each other across the line a visitor walks, or side by side. Do not
state the arrangement as fact in any painted detail.
