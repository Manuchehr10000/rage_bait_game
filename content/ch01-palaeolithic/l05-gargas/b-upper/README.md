# Beat b · the upper cave

Gargas II: narrow, three tiles clear, a clay roof. Lines drawn with the fingers in the
clay, in tangles, and a few painted animals on the wall under them: ibex and bison
silhouettes, as the first part of the visit shows them.

Thirty tiles, five seconds, and one thing to jump: **a bears' wallow in the floor**, two
tiles of clay crust under the second of the painted animals, with a shaft under it. A man
who walks on to it goes down it a sixth of a second later (`The bear nests`), three and a
half seconds after the door: the first lesson of the level, and a cheap one to repeat.

Every time after that it is **the place to put the lamp out**: the floor is flat, the roof
is flat, and five seconds walked in the spill of an unlit lamp is five seconds of light in
hand at the well — the difference between arriving there with a tenth of the battery and a
third, and between a second and a third of standing still before the lamp gives out and
more than six. But the painted animals are the only thing to count the wallow from, and with
the lamp out they are not there: the player who saves his light hops the wallow by counting.
The fastest run that never touches the switch does not need to; it only needs to be perfect
everywhere else.

**Must be right:** the upper cave is the narrow one; finger tracings on the clay roof; the
painted animals are few, black, and on the wall, not the roof.

**Deliberately wrong:** the animals are generic silhouettes at this size, not copies of
any published figure. The wallow: the sources put the bears' traces in the lower cave, the
wallows are shallow hollows and not a crust over a shaft, and its place is the level's.

No new art. The finger tracings and the animals are panels drawn by the game.
