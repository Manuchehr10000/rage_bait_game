# Beat d · the great hall, and the Camarin

The great hall of Gargas I: wide, five tiles clear, the height of a full jump. Claw marks
where the bears reached up. The block at the foot of the stair and the stalagmite the guide
calls a bear, `bear-stalagmite`, on the floor: a hop each.

**Three patches of the floor are not floor.** The bears left wallows in the clay of Gargas,
hollows where they slept, and each patch is the crust over one: two tiles of the same clay,
drawn standing on the same rock as the rest of the hall until the moment it goes, a sixth of
a second after he steps on it. There is a shaft under each and the bottom of it is the
oubliettes. The first is between the block and the bear, the second between the bear and
the Camarin, the third after it.

Then the Camarin. A hole in the floor three tiles wide and three deep. A full jump clears
it; a tap does not. From the bottom a passage runs back under the floor you came along,
four tiles, to a smooth wall with two engraved animals on it, the bull and the horse,
drawn as pale lines where the surface came off. Going in and coming out costs a couple of
seconds of the lamp, more if you look — or nothing, if he puts the lamp out and looks at
them by the spill round his feet, which is a worse look and a free one. From the hall floor
above, lit or not, the hole is a hole and nothing more.

**Must be right:** the Camarin is small and smooth-walled and holds most of the cave's
engravings; the Great Bull and Great Horse are its largest; the bears' claw marks and
wallows are real.

**Deliberately wrong:** the Camarin is a side chamber, not a pit and a passage under the
floor. The engravings are two beasts at this size, not copies. The wallows are shallow
hollows, not a crust over a drop, and the hall floor does not open under anybody.

The stalagmite has its own note. The claw marks, the hole, the false floors and the
engravings are drawn by the game.
