# Beat e · the well of the Oubliettes

Twelve tiles where the floor is gone, all the way down. Across it the fitted path: three
concrete slabs on their bearers, the middle one a step up. Falling in is `The oubliettes`.

**The middle slab tips.** It is not bearing on anything. A sixth of a second after he lands
on it, it goes off its bearer, the far end first, and it is not under him any more: whoever
is still on it goes down the well with it, not down on it. The first and third slabs are
further apart than a running jump carries, so the middle one has to be touched and left in
the same breath: a jump off it within about a quarter of a second of landing, or none.

The point of the beat is still the lamp. The fastest run that never touches the switch
arrives at the first slab as the flicker starts, so the jump on to the slab that tips is
made in a light that is going on and off on a quarter-second clock. A run that walked the
upper cave in the dark arrives with a third of the battery and a steady cone. A run that
went down into the Camarin with the lamp lit, or stood pinned on the ninth step for long
enough, arrives with nothing, and crosses in the spill round his feet and a dark in which
the slabs are a shade less black than the well.

**Must be right:** the well is deep and it is in the lower cave; the path is the modern
visit's, made and railed.

**Deliberately wrong:** the well is not on the route and the route does not cross it on
slabs, and nothing in the sources says any part of the real path has ever failed anybody.
The concrete is Pech Merle's. The name is the real one.

No new art. The slabs are the walkway pieces of Pech Merle, drawn by the game.
