# Beat c · the rhinos, the check rails, and the first hole in the bed

Above the track, the Frieze of Three Rhinos: three woolly rhinoceros in a line, in black,
each drawn round a nodule of flint that stands for the shoulder. The nodules in these
walls really do define where the drawings are.

Under the track, the level stops being about jumping.

**The check rails.** A check rail is a second rail laid inside the running rail, and the
slot between the two is a boot's width. There are four of them in this level; two are
track and two have a boot in them. They are drawn by the same code, they are the same
thirty-two pixels, and nothing about either kind says which it is. Stepping into one does
not hurt the tourist at all. It holds him exactly where he is, standing up, facing the way
he was going, and then the timetable arrives. It is the only death in the game that the
player watches coming for two whole seconds, and it is the level's best argument that the
train was always the thing that was going to kill him.

**The hole in the bed.** Forty-eight pixels of ballast that is not attached to anything
any more. Standing on it buys a sixth of a second. Under it is nothing at all, down to the
lower galleries. Over it, unchanged, are the rails: laid straight across the gap the way
rails are, because a rail is a beam and a beam holds up a train. The player who reads the
rails as a floor is reading them exactly as they are drawn and exactly as they are wrong.

Both are clear of the flint, because being caught is not being killed and a jump has to be
available.

No new art. The rails, the check rails and the ballast are drawn by the game.
