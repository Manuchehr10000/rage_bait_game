## What it is

A trackside stop board: a short steel post with a round plate on top, white with a red
ring and a red centre, weathered and a little dirty. The plate faces along the line. It
is the sort of thing that tells a driver where to halt, and it is the only piece of
signage in the cave.

## Where it stands in the game

Four of them on the bed, through beats b to e. Each is solid and eighteen pixels tall:
a jump gets over one, walking into one stops the tourist dead, and the train does not
stop. The last of the four has a signal lamp over it.

## Must be right

- Steel and enamel, not wood: this is railway equipment, not a road sign.
- Small. It is a narrow-gauge line in a cave and everything on it is undersized.
- Plain. No text, no numbers, no arrow (pillar 2).
- Dirty at the foot, where the ballast has thrown grit at it for sixty years.

## Deliberately wrong

That there are stop boards along the line at all, and that they stand in the middle of
the bed rather than beside it. The real train runs to a guide's timetable and stops
where he tells it.

## Sources

- Grotte de Rouffignac, official site: the visit is by electric train, about a kilometre
  in, since 1959.

## Confidence

The train and its date are solid. The boards are level design.
