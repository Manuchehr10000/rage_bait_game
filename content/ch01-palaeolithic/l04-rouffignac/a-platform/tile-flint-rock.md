## What it is

The Coniacian limestone the cave is cut through. Dry, bedded, and shot through with flint: nodules of it in near-horizontal bands, dark and glassy inside, weathering to a pale cortex on the outside. The Magdalenians drew round them and used them.

## Where it stands in the game

Every solid tile of roof and wall in the level. The game draws a nodule into every third course of tiles so the bands read as bands.

## Must be right

- Flint in **bands**, near-horizontal, not scattered.
- Each nodule pale on the outside, dark inside. Cortex is the tell.
- Dry. No dripstone, no wet sheen: this is not Pech Merle.
- Bedded, and the bedding roughly level.

## Deliberately wrong

Nothing. The rock is drawn as it is. Where the flint hangs low over the track is a matter of the roof geometry, not of this tile.

## Sources

- Show Caves of France, Grotte de Rouffignac: limestone "full of flint nodules", walls "almost vertical, with thick bands of brown flint nodules in almost horizontal layers".
- Bradshaw Foundation: the walls "inlaid with flint nodules randomly protruding and often defining the decorated areas".

## Confidence

The flint and its banding are well documented. The exact course spacing is drawn for the tile, not measured.
