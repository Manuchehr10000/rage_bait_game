## What it is

A plaster cast of the skeleton found in 1911 at the foot of the frieze: lying on its left side, knees drawn up, skull to the left, in a shallow hollow. 24 × 8, in its own hollow under the first horse, a tile above the floor of the trench. White plaster, a little grey in the hollows.

## Where it stands in the game

Beat c, under the first horse. Decor: it does nothing, and nothing happens near it. The
floor of the trench that kills begins past its hollow, and the first place anyone can come
off the frieze is past it too. Graves are never traps (`research/arc.md`).

## Must be right

- Flexed on the side, knees up. Not laid out straight.
- Plaster white, and visibly a cast: too clean for bone.

## Deliberately wrong

None that matters: it lies where the burial was found, and a cast is what is there.

## Sources

- Lalanne & Breuil, *L'abri sculpté de Cap-Blanc à Laussel (Dordogne)*, L'Anthropologie 22 (1911). Public domain. The first publication, with Breuil's drawings of the frieze.
- Cartailhac & Breuil, *La caverne d'Altamira* (1906), for Breuil's drawing conventions, from US-hosted scans only (EU copyright in the plates runs to the end of 2031).
- The Field Museum's record of the 1911 burial (their catalogue, not their photographs).
- Roussot, *Le Cap Blanc* (the site's own guide) for the layout of the shelter as visited. Reference only.
- Modern photographs for reference on your own screen only; the frieze is photographed under museum light and every one is in copyright.

## Confidence

The burial and its position are well documented; the sex and age are given differently by sources, so the note says a young adult.
