## What it is

A block of limestone off the roof of the shelter, falling. Roughly rectangular, broken on
every face, with one **fresh, pale face** where it has just parted from the overhang and
one **weathered** face that used to be the ceiling. 24 × 18.

## Where it stands in the game

Beat d, over the tenth horse, the last of the crossing. It starts just under the overhang and comes down on anyone
who has stood on that horse for a second. It lands on the horse's back and stays there.

## Must be right

- The same limestone as the shelter: cream, bedded, not granite and not sandstone.
- One face clean and one weathered. A block that has just fallen has not had time to
  weather where it broke.
- No moss, no soil. It came off a ceiling.

## Deliberately wrong

It waits for you. Roof fall does not take an interest in visitors, and a block that size
would have come down in the Magdalenian and been part of the deposit ever since.

## Easter eggs

Roof fall is what sealed the archaeology of Cap Blanc: the frieze was under a deposit of
it until 1909. The one thing in the dark half of the level that is telling the truth is
the thing that kills you.

## Sources

- Lalanne & Breuil, *L'abri sculpté de Cap-Blanc à Laussel (Dordogne)*, L'Anthropologie 22 (1911), for the section of the deposit.
- Any excavation report of a Dordogne shelter, for what éboulis looks like in section.

## Confidence

Roof fall and its role in shelter stratigraphy are well documented. The size and shape of
this particular block are design.
