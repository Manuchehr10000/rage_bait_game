# Beat d · the break

The fourth horse, with its muzzle gone. Everything of the head from the break line (sprite
column 38) is missing, and the break is **fresh stone**: pale, the colour limestone is
inside, not the weathered and ochred face of the rest of the horse. The game draws the
break over the horse sprite (`drawBreak` in `src/render/scene.ts`), so the fourth's break
and any break he makes himself at the fifth are the same break, line for line (pillar 4).
If `horse-relief` is painted, the break stays the game's.

**The trick: 'The muzzle'.** The three horses before it teach a man to jump from the tip of
the muzzle. Here the tip is air. The head ends 24 px short of where the tip was, farther
than the tenth of a second an edge forgives, and a man who jumps where the muzzle was runs
off the break into the trench. The fourth horse claims the death (pillar 8), though the
trench finishes him.

**Once known:** jump from the break. In practice, the moment he lands on the neck after
stepping down off the back. How far to jump is the next beat's business.

**Must be right:** the break is pale and ragged, a pick's break, not a saw cut. The rest
of the horse, ochre and all, is exactly the other four.

**Deliberately wrong:** the story at the site is that the first blow of the pick that found
the frieze, in 1909, took a muzzle off a horse. Which horse, and whether it was a muzzle at
all, is tradition, not record. The game gives it to the fourth.
