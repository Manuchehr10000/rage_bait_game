# Beat c · the hunt

One screen, 320 px, under the shaft. Everything in it, its positions, the cure, the pitfall, the
throw and the heights, is in `../LEVEL.md` (beat c) and ruled in `arc.md`, section 4. This note
is what must be drawn and what each thing is.

- **The Montespan bear** (56 to 76 px): headless, modelled in clay, lying with its forepaws
  forward and a bear's skull between them, spears standing in its body. He takes a spear from it
  by touching it with empty hands. It is a double too, and its double is the skull: pulling spears
  from it heals nothing.
- **The mammoth** (140 to 204 px, about 30 px tall): woolly, its domed head and shoulder hump the
  highest points, facing left, toward him. Blind at first: its eye shows shut. It backs, rump
  first, never turning. Nothing ever sticks in it; its eye only ever shows shut or open.
- **The strip** (204 to 216 px): plain floor between its rump and the cover.
- **The pit and its cover** (216 to 280 px, 32 px deep): poles and hides over a hole. It holds
  him; it goes under the beast's hind feet.
- **The far lip** (280 to 292 px), under the rock that leans over it to 24 px above its floor.
- **The picture**, on the face of the rock that closes the room (292 px on), over the pit:
  identical to the mammoth, pixel for pixel, its foot level with the lip's floor. On it, and only
  on it: the hunters' spear in its eye; the trap sign, a red roof, at 12 to 15 px, just below the
  heart; the heart, a natural hole in the rock, at 16 to 19 px; every spear he throws, where it
  sticks.

**Must be right:** the Montespan bear as Casteret found it, headless clay with a real bear's
skull between its paws and holes in its body; a woolly mammoth's build, domed head and shoulder
hump, sloping back; the trap sign drawn as the tectiforms are, roof-shaped, in red; the picture
drawn as a Breuil plate draws an animal; the room drawn as the plate.

**Deliberately wrong:** a live mammoth in a cave room; the pit, its cover and the leaning rock;
the bear and the mammoth in one room, from caves far apart; spears standing in the bear, where the
tellers read holes; the tectiform as a trap, which is the tellers' reading shown as true, as a
legend may (`arc.md`, section 4, 2026-10-28); the double, which is the story's.

**Never:** a hunter. The hunters are competent and never appear: their work is the spear in the
eye, the sign and the pit (pillar 11).

**Open, to check before drawing:** the bear's posture, size and holes, and where the skull lay;
which mammoth pictures carry tectiforms and where on the body; Bégouën's words on a natural hole as
a wound.
