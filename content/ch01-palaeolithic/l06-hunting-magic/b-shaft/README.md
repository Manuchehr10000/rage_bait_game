# Beat b · the shaft: the horse, the hind, the relief

A zigzag shaft one screen wide, 320 px, about 540 px deep, in four stages, drawn as a Breuil
plate of Altamira's Great Ceiling. Three of the ceiling's animals are on its walls, behind him:
the horse, the hind, the bison. Only the third is a trap. Numbers marked "model" are from a model
of `PHYS` and are pinned when the level is built (`../LEVEL.md`).

1. **The horse.** Off the first ledge, a fall of about 100 px past the horse, onto ledge 2.
2. **The hind.** A walk-off drift from ledge 2, about 80 px down and 40 across, through the hind,
   onto ledge A. A full jump meets the far wall and slides him down onto A. Nothing here teaches
   a jump: the habit is to walk off and drift through the picture.
3. **The bison: the relief** (counted). Over the gap just past A's lip, its back 20 px above A,
   32 px long, its belly 20 px below A, drawn flat as the horse and the hind are. It is painted on
   a boss the plate does not show, and the boss is solid. Ledge C is 150 px below, to the right,
   from about 40 px past A's lip and under the bison's far end; under the rest of the gap the
   shaft is open to the hunt room's floor, about 250 px down.
   - Walked off, or a tap of jump: he meets the flank side on, stops dead in the air and falls
     the open shaft to the hunt room's left edge, a fall's body there in front of the blind
     mammoth. The bison shows no mark of him. The trap claims the fall (`World.claim`): "The
     relief".
   - A full jump clears the back and dies of its own height on C, about 212 px: his own, uncounted.
   - A medium jump, about 3 to 12 frames of hold (model), lands on the back, a floor; he walks off
     it to C, about 170 px. Lever if it plays easy: a 24 px back.
4. **Down to the hunt.** From C a walk-off of about 100 px onto the hunt room's left floor.

No drop off stages 1, 2 and 4 is more than about 142 px (model): a miss there costs nothing.
Only stage 3 opens all the way down.

The setup is the medium, not a twin: the horse and the hind are different animals from the
bison, and the bison is the first bison, so the relief is not the touchstone cows' shape
(`content/tricks.md`). No picture here acts and nothing happens to any beast: the cure in beat c
is the level's first showing of the double.

**Must be right:** the horse, the hind and the bison are Altamira's, from the Great Ceiling, in
their poses there; the bison is one of the ceiling's polychrome bison, which are painted on
natural bulges of the rock; the plate is Breuil's manner, line and stipple, colour where his
plates carry it. The horse, the hind and the bison are drawn in one manner, so that nothing but
the bison's species marks it.

**Deliberately wrong:** the animals are on the walls of a shaft, not on a ceiling; the boss is on
a wall, since a boss over a jump is the flint lip; the boss is solid in a world drawn flat, and a
copy on paper is a world he walks in; the shaft and its four stages are the level's. Their sizes
are the level's, at about 9 px to the metre against him, except where the jump needs otherwise,
and the bison is sized to its stage (32 px long).

**Open, to check before drawing:** whether Breuil's plates mark relief at all. If they draw a
contour round a boss, this copy draws none, and that is the copy's lie, said here. The horse's and
the hind's poses on the ceiling, and which bison is drawn. Breuil's boars, read as bison by
Freeman (1987), are disputed; none of them is drawn.
