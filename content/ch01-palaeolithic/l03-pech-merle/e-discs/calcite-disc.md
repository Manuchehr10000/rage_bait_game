## What it is

A **disc**: one of the strangest things limestone does. Water carrying dissolved limestone
is forced under pressure through a narrow crack in the rock, sprays out in a fan, and sets
as a thin circular plate growing out of the crack. Pech Merle has a hall of them.

Drawn edge on, because that is how the level meets them: a plate with a flat top edge, a
wider face below it, and a narrowing where it goes back into the rock. Wet, pale, with
faint growth rings. 24 × 14; the top 6 px is the ledge.

## Where it stands in the game

Beat e, six of them across the hole where the floor is gone, at different heights. Two of
them give way a third of a second after you put your weight on one. There are no
footprints anywhere in this beat, because nobody walked this — which, after twenty seconds
of following a trail, is its own kind of information.

## Must be right

- **It grows out of a crack**, not off the floor and not off the ceiling. The plate is
  fed from its root and the root must be visible.
- **Thin.** A disc is a plate, a few centimetres through. It should look like something
  nobody would ever be allowed to stand on.
- Wet, pale, faintly banded with the rings of its own growth.
- No stalactite dripping off it and no stalagmite under it. A disc is not a dripstone
  formation and drawing it as one is the mistake a teacher would catch.

## Deliberately wrong

Two of them give way. Discs are fragile and are roped off from visitors precisely so that
nobody finds out, but they have been where they are for a very long time and they are not
about to drop. And of course the real ones are never in a line you could cross.
