# Easter eggs · Pech Merle

For the designer. None of it is stated anywhere in the game.

- **The prints cannot lie.** They are not placed by hand. The game lays a trail from the
  door to the exit and then deletes every print that has no floor under it, so a gap in
  the trail is a hole, always, everywhere, with no exceptions. Nobody will notice the rule
  and everybody will learn the habit.

- **Two prints face backwards**, at the edge in beat c, and they are the entire warning
  before the first betrayal. A player who dies there twice will go back and look at them.

- **No prints at the discs.** He did not come up that way. After half a minute of a
  trail, its absence is information, and it is the only beat in the level with none.

- **The hollows do nothing.** Four bear nests, drawn exactly as a bear leaves them, in the
  one stretch of the level where every other dark shape in the floor will kill you.

- **The walkway is beautifully made.** It should be: it is real, it is inspected, and
  seven hundred people a day walk it safely. Everything wrong with it in this level is
  where the level put it, not how it is painted. Paint it well and it does its job.

- **The art is never a floor.** Two levels have taught the player that a carving holds
  their weight. This cave is painted, not carved, and nothing on the wall will take them.

- **The last trap is at the exit.** The tourist dies looking at the Spotted Horses from
  the spot the guide takes you to, which is the best view in the cave.

- **Three teenagers found this place in 1922** — André David, Henri Dutertre and Marthe
  David — and the priest who helped them through it spent the rest of his life publishing
  it. None of them is in the level. The level has no people in it at all.
