# Beat a · down into the cave

The honest opening and the last of the daylight. The tourist goes down, the dark closes,
and the headlamp comes on — which after Roc-aux-Sorciers, where it did nothing for two
thirds of a level, is the first time in the chapter it has been the only light there is.

Two things are laid out here and they agree with each other, which is the whole point of
the beat: **the concrete of the guided tour**, poured in slabs with a steel handrail on
stanchions, and **the prints of a boy in the clay beside it**. For the next twenty
seconds they go the same way. The player learns to read both, and is meant to end up
trusting the one that is lit, railed, level and modern.

The prints in this level are laid by rule and not by hand: a print only exists where there
is floor under it. Where the trail stops, the floor stops. Nothing else in the cave tells
you that.

Art here is the tile set — `tile-cave-rock`, `tile-clay`, `tile-concrete` and their tops —
and `footprint`. The handrail is drawn by the game.
