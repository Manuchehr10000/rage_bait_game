## What it is

A bare human footprint pressed into soft clay and then sealed under a thin crust of
calcite, which is what preserved it and what makes it pale against the floor. Small: the
prints at Pech Merle are an adolescent's. Toes at the front, a narrow waist, a heel. 6 × 5.

## Where it stands in the game

Everywhere the floor is solid and nowhere else. The game lays a trail the length of the
cave and then deletes every print that would be over a hole, so the gaps in the trail are
the holes. The player who reads the floor lives; the player who reads the handrail does
not.

## Must be right

- **Pale, not white.** A calcite skin over clay, catching a lamp. It is the brightest
  thing on the floor and it must not look painted on.
- The **shape of a bare foot** in soft ground: the toes spread, the arch does not touch.
  Not a boot, not a modern print.
- **Small.** These are a young person's prints and that is the single most affecting fact
  about the cave. If they read as a man's, the beat loses.
- The calcite crust is what proves they are Palaeolithic. A little of it should show.

## Deliberately wrong

There are twelve prints at Pech Merle, in one stretch of one gallery. The game lays a
hundred and fifty of them from the door to the exit, because the trail has to do the work
that a sign would do and there are no signs (pillar 2).

## Confidence

The prints, their number, their preservation under calcite, their position and the two
directions are documented. That they are an adolescent's is the published reading. The
shape drawn here is generic and is not a copy of any one of them.
