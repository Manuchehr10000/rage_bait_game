# Beat d · the Bear's Gallery

No tour comes down here. The concrete stops at the Chapel and does not start again until
the last chamber, and for the whole of this gallery the only things telling the tourist
where the floor is are a line of prints and a lamp that reaches about a body's length.

**Six shelves, stepping down and to the right**, over the lower gallery. Every one of
them is the same sixty-four pixels of the same clay, drawn by the same code as the floor
it broke off (pillar 4), and the spaces between them go all the way down. Three carry
prints. Three do not, and those three are:

- **the second will not let him stand still.** It is wet, and it carries whoever lands on
  it off its far end at a good deal more than walking pace. The gap after it is forty-eight
  pixels, which is further than being carried off the edge gets you and well inside a
  jump, so the only thing to do on it is leave it at once.
- **the fourth lets go**, a whole second after he lands on it.
- **the fifth walks.** Stand on it and it carries him forward at exactly his own running
  speed until it is hard against the sixth, and then it stops and stays there. It is a
  ledge that moves and a ledge that keeps him.

And then the trap the beat is built to set up. **The sixth holds for anybody who walks on
to it and will not take a landing.** The fifth delivers him to it on his feet; a man who
does what the second level spent its whole flat run teaching — leave anything that moves,
at once — jumps off the fifth, lands on the sixth, and goes through it. The prints are on
the sixth, and the prints are not lying: the boy walked there.

Cave bears wintered in galleries like this and scraped themselves hollows to do it in,
and the nests survive in the clay of a good many French caves. **The hollows here are
harmless**, which is what a bear nest is: a shallow bowl about thirty centimetres deep.
There are three of them, on the first, the fourth and the sixth — one that holds, one
that lets go and one that minds — so that a hollow means nothing whatever. What kills you
is the cave, not the bears.

## Deliberately wrong

Clay shelves do not slide, walk, let go or take offence. The descent, its spacing and
everything each shelf does are level design. The hollows, and that they are harmless,
are not.
