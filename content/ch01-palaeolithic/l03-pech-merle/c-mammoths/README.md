# Beat c · the Chapel of the Mammoths, and the first shaft

The clay of the upper gallery stops. The concrete does not: it runs five tiles out over
the shaft and ends in mid air, with three hundred and fifty pixels of nothing under the
end of it. This is the level's first sentence and it is the whole argument: **the made
path is the wrong one**.

The prints say so first. They run along the gallery, come to the lip of the shaft, and
**turn back** — two of them face the way he came, and then the trail starts again down
the far side. The boy walked to the edge, looked at the hole, and went round. Twenty-five
thousand years later somebody poured a slab out over it.

Four steps down the side of the shaft, all four the same sixty-four pixels of the same
clay, and the prints are on the first and the last:

- **the first holds.**
- **the second lets go**, half a second after he stands on it. Long enough to believe it
  is not going to.
- **the third lifts him.** It rises, sixty-four pixels, at the speed of a slow lift, and
  where it stops it lets go of him — so the one thing in the cave that carries him
  upwards is the thing that drops him furthest. There are no prints on it, and there is
  no other warning.
- **the fourth holds**, and it is the floor of the Bear's Gallery.

The Chapel itself — seven metres of mammoths and bison — is on the wall above all this,
where it is at Pech Merle, and it is never a floor.

## Deliberately wrong

A visitor path does not stop over a hole; it goes round, or there is no path. Clay
ledges do not let go and none of them has ever risen. The Chapel is in the upper gallery
and it does not overhang a shaft arranged in four tidy steps.
