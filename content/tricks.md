# Tricks: what the game has spent

Pillar 4: **a trick is used once in the whole game.** Chapters are designed in separate
conversations, so this file is how each one knows what the others have spent. Before a
trap is designed, look for it here. When a level is built or rebuilt, add its tricks.
Adding a trick someone else has already claimed is the one thing this file exists to stop.

## What counts as the same trick

A player who has met the first would recognise the second as the same thing again: the
same shape of setup and the same punchline, from the player's seat, whatever the art, the
site or the noun on the label. The body does not count: a new trick may end in an old
death, and a stone and an obelisk can both leave the same pancake (pillar 8). A different
fuse does not count either, unless it asks the player for a different answer. A trick the
player has met may come back only as the setup for a new one; list it under both.

## What a trick is for

The fun is curiosity: the player keeps going to find out how else the game will kill
him. The designer's two touchstones, from the conversation of 2026-09-26:

- **The obelisk.** It cracks as you come up to it. You brace for it to fall on you, and it
  does not. You pass it, and it comes down on you from behind. The setup is honest; the
  punchline is *when*. Once known, you have to outrun its length: a test of the hands.
- **The cows.** The first two hold, the third drops you, the fourth holds, and now you have
  a theory. The fifth kills you in a way you have not seen: a stone, landing where a player
  who learned from the third comes down, hopping off fast. A short hop lives. The lesson of
  the third cow is what kills you at the fifth.

Every trick passes three tests (pillar 4): a surprise the first time; timing or precision
the second, never only the memory of where; and it could only happen at this site.

later and were not in it) found 67 tricks, and 29 of them were a trick the player had
already met. Three reviewers judged each repeat; these are the ones at least two of the
three called the same trick. When a level is rebuilt its entries leave this list, and
each trick here may then be claimed once, by one level.
