# Level 1 · The Great Temple of Abu Simbel

## The site

Two rock-cut temples on the west bank of the Nile in Nubia, about 280 km south of Aswan, cut for Ramesses II between roughly 1264 and 1244 BC. The Great Temple is dedicated to Amun-Ra, Ra-Horakhty, Ptah and the deified Ramesses himself; the Small Temple, 100 m north, to Hathor and Queen Nefertari. The facade of the Great Temple is a pylon shape carved into the cliff, about 33 m high and 38 m wide, with four seated colossi of the king about 20 m tall. Inside, a hall with eight Osiride pillars leads 65 m into the rock to a sanctuary with four seated statues.

The sand covered it for most of two thousand years. Johann Ludwig Burckhardt saw the tops of the colossi in 1813; Giovanni Belzoni dug his way to the door in 1817. When Lake Nasser began to rise behind the Aswan High Dam, UNESCO led the campaign of 1964–68 that sawed both temples into blocks and rebuilt them 65 m higher and about 210 m back from the water, inside an artificial hill of concrete domes covered with rock. The reassembly is accurate to within centimetres; the joins are still visible as thin lines if you look.

## Details a teacher will look for

- The colossi wear the **nemes with the double crown** on top, a **uraeus** on the brow and the **false beard**. Cartouches of Ramesses on the chest and upper arms.
- At the legs of each colossus stand small figures of the family: Queen Nefertari, the king's mother Tuya, and sons and daughters including Amun-her-khepeshef, Ramesses, Bintanath, Baketmut, Nefertari and Meritamen. They reach roughly the king's knee.
- Over the door, in a niche, **Ra-Horakhty** stands with a falcon head and sun disc. In the real niche he holds a **user** sceptre and a figure of **Maat**, which with the sun (Ra) spells the king's throne name User-Maat-Ra. That rebus is the single most teacher-pleasing detail on the facade.
- Above the cornice, the **22 baboons**. Below the colossi, a terrace.
- Between the second and third colossi, the doorway, with a small cornice.
- Inside: the hall's **Osiride pillars** show the king wrapped as Osiris with crook and flail. The north wall carries the **Battle of Kadesh** (1274 BC) in sunk relief: the camp, the chariot charge, the king alone against the Hittites.
- In the sanctuary, left to right: **Ptah, Amun-Ra, Ramesses II, Ra-Horakhty**. The order matters.
- The sun alignment dates are usually given as 21 February and 21 October before the move and a day later after it, because the temple now sits at a slightly different bearing.

## Deliberate lies

- The ankh block.
- The fourth head falls on you. No head has fallen since antiquity.
- The baboon's date.
- The blocks go up on cables and the lake comes up in seconds. The real move took four years and the lake rose over a decade.
- The sun beam sweeps in as a wall of light and burns. The real light is a soft rectangle that lasts about twenty minutes.

## Confidence

Dates of construction, the four gods and their order, the 22 baboons, the broken second colossus, the sun alignment and the relocation figures are all well documented. The exact list and placement of the family figures at each colossus varies between sources; check against Lepsius before painting names onto anything.
