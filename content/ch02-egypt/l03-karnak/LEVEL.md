# Level 3 · Karnak

## The site

The temple complex of Amun-Ra at Thebes, east bank, built and rebuilt for about two thousand years from the Middle Kingdom (Senusret I, about 1950 BC) to the Ptolemies. About thirty pharaohs added to it. The main axis runs from the river through ten pylons. The level walks it from the outside in and then out again to the sacred lake:

- The **avenue of ram-headed sphinxes** (criosphinxes) of Ramesses II in front of the first pylon, each ram holding a small statue of the king between its forepaws. (The long avenue to Luxor is lined with human-headed sphinxes of Nectanebo I; ours are the rams at the gate.)
- The **first pylon**, the largest in Egypt at 113 m wide, never finished: it was built by the 30th Dynasty and left without its facing and its reliefs. Against its inner face the **mud-brick construction ramp** still stands, the only surviving example of how pylons were built.
- The **second pylon**, built by Horemheb, is filled with **talatat**: small standard blocks (about 52 × 26 × 22 cm, one cubit long) from the dismantled temples of **Akhenaten**, reused as rubble. Tens of thousands have been pulled out; many carry fragments of Aten reliefs.
- The **Great Court**, with the single standing column of the **Kiosk of Taharqa** (21 m).
- The **Hypostyle Hall**: 134 columns in 16 rows over about 5,000 square metres. The twelve columns of the central nave are 21 m tall with **open papyrus** capitals; the other 122 are about 15 m with **closed bud** capitals. Begun under Seti I and finished by Ramesses II. On 3 October 1899 eleven of them fell over. Since the 1960s the hall hosts a sound-and-light show, which is where the game's spotlights come from.
- The **obelisks**: Thutmose I's (about 22 m) and **Hatshepsut's** (29.5 m, about 320 tonnes, pink Aswan granite, cut in seven months according to its own inscription). The top of Hatshepsut's fallen second obelisk lies near the sacred lake.
- The **sacred lake**, about 120 × 77 m, stone-lined, with steps down to the water; the priests washed in it. At its north-west corner stands the **giant scarab** of Amenhotep III on a pedestal, moved here from his mortuary temple on the west bank.
- The **Karnak Cachette**: a pit in the court of the seventh pylon where Georges Legrain found, from 1903, about 750 stone statues and 17,000 bronzes that priests had buried in antiquity to clear the temple. The ankh block at the start of the level is over it.

## Details a teacher will look for

- **Ram heads** on the sphinxes, with the small **Ramesses statue between the paws**. Curled horns of the Amun ram (*Ovis platyra*), not a goat.
- The pylon is **unfinished**: rough, uncoursed face, no reliefs, and the ramp still against it.
- **Talatat** are small and uniform, and some carry cut-off pieces of Aten rays ending in hands.
- Hypostyle columns: **papyrus bundles**, with the open umbel capital in the nave and the closed bud in the aisles, abacus blocks above, and **clerestory windows** with stone grilles.
- The obelisk is **pink granite** with a **pyramidion** at the top (originally sheathed in electrum) and one column of hieroglyphs down each face.
- The sacred lake has **stone steps** into the water and a stone quay; a **nilometer** stair on one side.
- The scarab is on a **tall pedestal** at the lake's corner.

## Deliberate lies

- Everything that moves: rams, blocks, columns, obelisk, scarab, stones. Karnak has stood still for three thousand years, apart from 1899.
- The turnstile. The trapdoor. The spotlights.
- The water is swimmable and safe. It was a sacred lake; priests bathed in it, tourists are not meant to.

## Confidence

Column counts and heights, the obelisk dimensions, the 1899 collapse, the Cachette, the scarab, the talatat and the construction ramp are well documented. The exact number of criosphinxes in front of the first pylon today varies by source (several were moved in antiquity and in modern times).
