# Beat c · the east Osiris chapels, and the zodiac

The stair comes out onto the roof, into the open air for the first time since the gate, with
the ground fourteen tiles down on every side.

On the roof stand two suites of chapels for the mysteries of Osiris, one on the east side and
one on the west. Each is an open court and then two roofed rooms. The game draws a suite as a
low court wall, the court, and the two rooms as one block a hop high; the tourist goes over
the wall, across the court, and up onto the rooms' roofs.

**Half the ceiling of the second room of the east suite was the Dendera zodiac:** a
sandstone block of about 2.55 by 2.53 metres carved on its underside with what is often
called the only complete circular map of the sky left from ancient Egypt. In 1821 Lelorrain, working for the collector
Saulnier, cut it out of the ceiling with saws, jacks, chisels and gunpowder. It was in Paris
the next year; it is in the Louvre now, and a plaster cast fills the hole.

From above, it was a roof slab like the one next to it, because the carving is on the other
side. In the game it is two tiles of the roof like all the others, and there is nothing to
tell them apart (pillar 4); the charge takes them out of the grid when it goes. **Land on it, walking or jumping, and a charge
goes off under it at once.** The slab is gone and the tourist is ash. **Death label: "The
zodiac".** The second attempt jumps it.

That jump is what beat d is built on.

No assets of its own: the chapels and the slab are `tile-dendera-sandstone` and its `-top`,
in `a-east-gate`.
