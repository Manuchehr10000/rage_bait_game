## What it is

One of the twelve columns of the kiosk in the south-west corner of the roof: a shaft, and on
it the sistrum capital that is Hathor's own emblem — the goddess's face with cow's ears, and on
her head a little shrine, the shape of the sistrum she is shaken with. The game draws four, the
back row as seen across the kiosk, with the architrave across their tops.

## Where it stands in the game

The last thing in the level. The top of the painting is the underside of the architrave; the
bottom edge is the roof.

## Must be right

- The sistrum capital: face, cow's ears, the shrine above.
- The screen walls between the columns reach about halfway up them.
- The architraves carry sockets that suggest a timber roof once sat on them. The game draws
  the sockets and no roof.
- All the columns the game draws are this painting (pillar 4). They do nothing, but a column
  painted differently from the others is read as a marker.

## Deliberately wrong

- The column's height is the game's: four columns 80 px high, over a kiosk eight tiles across.
- **Whether the faces on these capitals were hacked out is not checked.** Most of the faces in
  the outer hypostyle were. The game draws the face plain, with no features, so that it is not
  wrong either way. Look before painting a face on it, and look before painting one hacked.

## Sources

- Ancient Egypt Online, the kiosk in the south-west corner:
  https://ancientegyptonline.co.uk/hathortempledendera/
- David Roberts, *Egypt and Nubia*, "Temple of Isis on the roof of the great temple of
  Dendera" (1838). Public domain. The title is Roberts' mistake: it is the Hathor kiosk, and he
  also puts it in the wrong corner.

## Confidence

Twelve Hathor columns, the south-west corner, the New Year ritual, the sockets: solid. The
state of the faces: to be checked.
