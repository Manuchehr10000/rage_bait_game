## What it is

One figure of the New Year procession carved on the walls of the east stair, in sunk
relief, facing **left**: down the stair, toward the tourist coming up it. The game places one
on the back wall of every step, standing on the register line 4 px above the tread. Frame 0
is a priest; frame 1 a standard-bearer with his standard, which is what the 26 px of height
leave room for above the head. The game uses frame 1 on every third step.

## Where it stands in the game

Fourteen of them, one to a step, up the whole of beat b. They do nothing. They are the joke
that needs no words: he is going up the stair they are coming down.

## Must be right

- They face down the stair. That is the whole point of them.
- Sunk relief, cut into the wall, not painted on it and not standing out from it.
- Egyptian convention: head, legs and feet in profile, shoulders frontal.
- The procession on the real stair has the king, priests, singers and standard-bearers, and
  the shrine carried among them. Two frames cannot hold all of that; ask for more frames if
  the painter wants to paint the procession as it is. Nothing in it moves, so the figures do
  not have to be identical (pillar 4 is about things that behave).

## Deliberately wrong

- One figure to a step, and a figure the height of the tourist, is the game's spacing.
- The code draws priests and standard-bearers only.
