# Beat b · the east stair, and the ankh in its ceiling

Dendera has two stairs to the roof. The west one winds round a square core, about 110 steps,
and the New Year procession went **up** it with the goddess. The east one is straight, about
97 steps, and the procession came back **down** it. The reliefs on the walls of each stair
show the procession going that stair's way.

The tourist goes up the east one. It is the one a side view can draw, and it is the wrong
one. Every figure carved on the wall beside him is walking down the stair toward him. A
teacher sees it at once; the game says nothing.

The stair is the level's first roof over his head. Fourteen steps, a tile each, with three
tiles of head room over every tread: a full jump off any step meets the ceiling above it and
does no harm; a short hop clears the next step and touches nothing. The first seven steps
teach that bumping your head in here costs nothing.

Over the eighth step, one block of the ceiling is the chapter's ankh block, the one
anachronism, the thing from a more famous game that gave a coin at Abu Simbel and opened the
floor at Karnak. A full jump off the eighth step hits it. It pays its coin, the way it always
has, and then it comes out of the ceiling and down on him. **Death label: "Ankh".** A hop
short enough to clear the step and not reach the ceiling goes under it.

This is the one beat whose trap owes nothing to the site. The site's part of it is the stair,
the ceiling and the procession; the ankh is the game's.

The procession figure has its own note. The stair, its ceiling and the head of the stair
standing up out of the roof are tiles; the back wall of the flight is drawn by the game.
