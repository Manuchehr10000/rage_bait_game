# Level 2 · Philae, the Temple of Isis

## The site

An island in the Nile just above the First Cataract at Aswan, and the last great temple of the old religion. The Temple of Isis was built mostly by the Ptolemies from about 280 BC, with Roman additions: the unfinished **Kiosk of Trajan** (about AD 100) with its fourteen columns and composite capitals, and the **Gate of Hadrian**. On the Gate of Hadrian someone cut the **last hieroglyphic inscription anywhere**, dated 24 August AD 394; the last demotic graffito is from AD 452. The temple stayed open, by treaty with the Nubian Blemmyes, until Justinian closed it around AD 535–537 and it became a church. Crosses were cut into the reliefs and the faces of the gods hacked out.

After the first Aswan Dam (1902) the island flooded for most of every year and tourists rowed over the submerged temple, looking down at the columns through the water. After the High Dam it would have drowned for good. In 1972–80 a **cofferdam** of two rows of steel sheet piling filled with sand was built around the island, the water pumped out, and the temples cut into about 40,000 blocks and rebuilt on the nearby island of **Agilkia**, landscaped to look like Philae.

## Details a teacher will look for

- **Sandstone**, warm, not grey. Only the bedrock and the landing stages are granite.
- The Kiosk's columns carry **composite floral capitals** (papyrus, lotus and palm mixed, in the Ptolemaic manner) topped by tall **abacus blocks** that were meant to be carved into Hathor heads and never were. Roberts' famous view shows the blocks plain.
- The **first pylon** shows Ptolemy XII Neos Dionysos smiting enemies before Isis, Horus and Hathor.
- The **Coptic crosses** cut into the pylon and the colonnade, and the hacked faces of Isis. The sixth century did that, not time.
- The **last hieroglyph** is on the Gate of Hadrian, by a priest of Isis named Esmet-Akhom, next to a figure of the god Mandulis. Dated to the birthday of Osiris in the year 110 of Diocletian.
- Feluccas: a lateen sail on a raked mast, a low hull, a tiller.

## Deliberate lies

- The crocodile that is a rock. The relief that steps out.
- Blocks 201–206 do nothing; the ground next to them sinks. The real relocation blocks did not sit next to a sinking bank.
- The cofferdam bursts. It never did.
- The water inside the Kiosk rises while you climb. The real flooding was seasonal and slow.

## Confidence

Dates, the last inscription, the closure under Justinian, the cofferdam and the move to Agilkia are well documented. Whether crocodiles were common at Philae specifically, as opposed to the cataract region generally, is not something to state as fact.
